How this was built

Every roster was played through — simulated weeks of box scores for each injury setting, using the draft board's projections: per-possession rates from three seasons, minutes from depth charts, and projected games played. Each NBA team plays a three- or four-game week most of the time, and an injured player misses whole weeks. Makes and attempts are simulated separately, so FG% and FT% come out of real shot volume. Each pair of teams is compared week by week, category by category.

  • Rosters are as drafted, plus your Mitchell Robinson pickup with Brandon Ingram on IL. Other managers' moves since the draft aren't included. Kevin Porter Jr. (OJ7) was waived by the Bucks and is treated as a waiver pickup.
  • Missed weeks. With — IL slots and — adds a week, managers replace long injuries with waiver players. The setting above controls how often: none, half or all of the time, with the replacement playing like an average top-40 free agent.
  • — rookies with no NBA history use stand-ins: the average projected player at the same position drafted around the same spot.
  • Veteran minutes. Five players kept the board's flat starter minutes despite a recent drop; here they get a blend of their last two seasons. Only Brook Lopez and Tobias Harris change much, and both are undrafted.
  • Not modelled: the real NBA schedule (a four-game week against a three-game week matters a lot in that week), the daily cap of 11 starters, and projection error itself. The gaps between teams are small, so treat close matchups as coin flips.
